Dividing money across stocks involves conflicting goals: high return, low volatility and limited losses in bad periods. This project, SwarmFolio, treats long-only portfolio selection as a three-objective problem (annual return, annual volatility and Conditional Value-at-Risk) with a cap on each holding, a minimum holding size and an optional limit on the number of holdings. A multi-objective Particle Swarm Optimizer and an NSGA-II genetic algorithm are built on one shared repair operator, so that both search the same feasible space and can be compared fairly. The system is delivered as a Python library, a command-line tool and a browser workbench that animates both populations converging on their Pareto fronts and lets the user inspect any portfolio on them.

Six experiments on seeded simulated markets show that both algorithms find much better fronts than random search at an equal budget (hypervolume about 0.70 against 0.25) and never violate a constraint. They are close to each other: the swarm converges in far fewer iterations and gives a more even front, while the genetic algorithm ends marginally ahead from about 100 iterations. In 30 independent markets, portfolios chosen for the best Sharpe ratio fell from about 1.34 in training to about 0.53 on held-out data, no better than equal weight (0.59), whereas minimum-volatility portfolios had lower held-out volatility than equal weight in all 30 markets. A good front is therefore no promise of future return, and risk-oriented choices from the front are the more dependable.
